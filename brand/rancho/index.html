The family, and why it exists

The two brand colours cannot carry text on the dark ground. Red manages 3.89:1, which is large text only. Blue manages 1.57:1, which is unreadable. These steps exist so the brand can be used in interfaces without failing anybody.

There is no pale end to the red. Adding white to Jaramillo Red gives pink, salmon or coral, and none of those is a brand colour, however subtle the step looks on its own. When red needs to be readable it gets darker or it gets a different ground, never lighter. The build refuses to ship any red carrying more than a fifth white, which is the only reason this rule holds.

Typeface

RANCHO JARAMILLO

The wordmark is a classical Roman capital in the Trajan manner: high stroke contrast, fine bracketed serifs, no lowercase. Cinzel is the closest freely licensed match and is what this page is set in, so the web can carry the same voice as the artwork. Set it in capitals only, with generous letter spacing, and never below about 14px.

Everything that is not a heading stays in Archivo, the face the rest of the estate uses. The Roman capital is for the name and for display, not for reading.

Rules

Do Keep the background transparent, always. Cut favicons from the mark alone. Use Red 300 for text on dark and Red 700 on paper. Give the lockup clear space of at least the height of the horns on every side.
Do not Put the lockup on a white plate. Set text in Blue 500 on the dark ground, at any size. Use the full lockup below about 120px wide, where the wordmark closes up. Recolour the horns, or separate the red from the blue.
Colours sampled directly from the artwork Oscar supplied: the median of every opaque pixel in each region of the mark. Contrast figures are WCAG 2.1 ratios against #0A1523 and #FAF8F4. The supplied artwork is raster, so the lockup is sharp to roughly 900px wide. For a step and repeat, a banner or vehicle livery, ask Oscar for the vector.
